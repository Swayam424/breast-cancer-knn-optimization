\begin{abstract}
\textit{The $k$-nearest-neighbours (KNN) rule is among the simplest and most transparent non-parametric classifiers: it stores the training set and assigns to an unlabelled query the majority label of its closest samples. When deployed in clinical oncology, however, classical KNN faces three severe obstacles: (i) raw cellular feature spaces exhibit uneven dimensional variances that distort Euclidean distance; (ii) hard majority voting yields zero gradients everywhere, preventing representation learning via modern optimizers; and (iii) standard 0-1 classification loss treats life-threatening false negatives (missed malignancies) identically to false positives. This paper presents a systematic, fully reproducible optimization study that transforms classical KNN into a high-performance diagnostic system on the Wisconsin Diagnostic Breast Cancer (WDBC) benchmark. The progression advances through four stages: (i) principal component analysis compresses the 30-D input to 10 dimensions while retaining 95.3\% of total morphological variance, coupled with SIMD vectorization that accelerates CPU query latency by 122$\times$; (ii) we introduce Soft-KNN, a differentiable reformulation where hard voting is replaced by a temperature-scaled softmax over negative distances to class anchor banks, enabling backpropagation through a metric backbone trained with AdamW; (iii) clinical cost-sensitive calibration lowers the decision threshold from 4/7 to 3/7 votes, successfully diagnosing Patient \#35 (a confirmed malignant carcinoma missed by standard majority voting); and (iv) multi-seed ensembling and convex simplex fusion consolidate intermediate representations to attain 97.37\% test accuracy with 97.6\% malignant recall. All 15 experiments are recorded in a machine-readable ledger, negative results are reported with equal scientific rigor, and the complete pipeline reproduces end-to-end from a standalone Google Colab notebook.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Problem Statement}
K-nearest neighbors is the archetypal \textit{lazy learner}: it stores the training partition and defers all computation to query time, classifying a sample by a majority vote among its $k$ closest neighbors. Its appeal lies in conceptual transparency and the absence of an explicit training phase; its foundational guarantee is established by the celebrated \textbf{Cover-Hart Theorem (1967)} \cite{cover1967}, proving that the asymptotic 1-NN error rate $R$ is bounded by the Bayes optimal error $R^*$ as:
\begin{equation}
R^* \le R \le 2R^*(1 - R^*)
\end{equation}

When applied to diagnostic oncology, however, classical KNN encounters structural obstacles. In the Wisconsin Diagnostic Breast Cancer (WDBC) benchmark \cite{wolberg1995}, 30 continuous nuclear morphological features differ across four orders of magnitude (e.g., cell nucleus area $\approx 2500\text{ mm}^2$ versus smoothness $\approx 0.08$). Without normalization, large features completely overwhelm the metric. Furthermore, hard voting yields zero gradients everywhere, preventing metric optimization via first-order gradient descent. Finally, in oncology, missing a malignant tumor (False Negative) is potentially fatal, whereas a false alarm (False Positive) is non-fatal. Standard symmetric accuracy obscures this asymmetric clinical reality.

\paragraph{Research Questions.} How far can KNN be elevated on continuous cellular biopsy data through (i) principled metric transformations and SIMD tensor vectorization, (ii) a differentiable Soft-KNN reformulation that makes the metric space trainable by backpropagation, and (iii) clinical cost-sensitive threshold calibration?

\section{Dataset and Experimental Protocol}
\paragraph{Dataset.} The Wisconsin Diagnostic Breast Cancer (WDBC) dataset comprises 569 patient biopsy samples (357 Benign, 212 Malignant) with 30 nuclear morphological features.
\paragraph{Protocol Rules.} (i) Stratified 80/20 train/test partition (\texttt{seed=42}); (ii) \texttt{StandardScaler} is fitted strictly on the training partition to eliminate data leakage; (iii) the hidden test set is evaluated once per reported experiment; (iv) all results are logged to a versioned machine-readable JSON ledger.

\section{K-Nearest Neighbors: Foundations}
\subsection{Distance Metrics on Continuous Cellular Features}
Given normalized vectors $x, z \in \mathbb{R}^{30}$, we evaluate four canonical dissimilarities:
\begin{align}
d_2(x, z) &= \sqrt{\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{30} (x_i - z_i)^2} \quad \text{(Euclidean)} \\
d_1(x, z) &= \sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{30} |x_i - z_i| \quad \text{(Manhattan)} \\
d_{\text{cos}}(x, z) &= 1 - \frac{\langle x, z \rangle}{\|x\|_2 \|z\|_2} \quad \text{(Cosine)} \\
d_M(x, z) &= \sqrt{(x - z)^T \Sigma^{-1} (x - z)} \quad \text{(Mahalanobis)}
\end{align}

\subsection{Curse of Dimensionality and SIMD Vectorization}
In high dimensions, pairwise distance contrast collapses ($d_{\max}/d_{\min} \to 1$). PCA spectral compression demonstrates that $d=10$ components retain \textbf{95.3\% cumulative variance}. By implementing pairwise distance via NumPy multidimensional array broadcasting:
\begin{equation}
D_{ij} = \sqrt{\sum\nolimits_{m=1}^{30} (X^{\text{te}}_{im} - X^{\text{tr}}_{jm})^2}
\end{equation}
Inference latency drops from 1974.8 ms to 16.18 ms—a \textbf{122$\times$ wall-clock acceleration} executing directly in CPU vector registers.

\section{Soft-KNN: A Differentiable Reformulation}
Following differentiable metric learning \cite{goldberger2004,weinberger2009}, we map inputs through an MLP metric head $z = f_\theta(x) / \|f_\theta(x)\|_2$. Defining the mean distance to the $k$ nearest class anchors as $d_c(z) = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}_k^{(c)}} \|z - z_i\|_2$, the class posterior is:
\begin{equation}
P(y=c \mid x) = \frac{\exp(-d_c(z) / \tau)}{\sum_j \exp(-d_j(z) / \tau)}
\end{equation}
Trained with AdamW ($\tau=0.1$), Soft-KNN reaches \textbf{96.49\% test accuracy} and \textbf{97.62\% malignant recall}.
